A practical first guide

Start small.
Build at your pace.

Self-hosting is running an app or service on hardware you control, instead of relying only on a hosted platform. You can start with one useful app and learn from there.

Find an app for you Browse the catalog
One step at a time

How to find your first project.

01

Pick something you already use

Photos, movies, files, or a smart-home service are all good starting points. Choose a problem you actually want to solve.

02

Compare a few projects

Read the official project documentation, check how active the project is, and understand its features and limitations.

03

Check requirements before buying hardware

Look at CPU, memory, storage, and supported architecture. Also consider ongoing costs such as electricity, a domain, or a rented server.

04

Plan backups, updates, and access

Decide how you’ll protect your data, keep the app updated, and connect to it. Don’t expose a service to the public internet until you understand how to secure and maintain it.

05

Try one app and learn by doing

Follow the app’s current installation guide, test it with non-critical data, and make sure you know how to restore a backup before relying on it.

Choose a home for your apps

Hardware that fits your setup.

There’s no universal best option. Match your hardware to the apps, storage, reliability, and maintenance you need.

LOW POWER

Raspberry Pi or small device

Useful for lighter services, but check memory, processor, and storage requirements first.

Examples to exploreVaultwarden, Pi-hole or AdGuard Home, Uptime Kuma, Syncthing, Portainer.

Resource-heavy apps such as Immich or a full Home Assistant OS appliance may need more capable hardware.

AT HOME

Mini PC, NAS, or old desktop

A flexible option when you want more room for storage or several services. Check power use, drive health, and how you’ll handle backups.

Examples to exploreImmich, Jellyfin, Nextcloud, and Home Assistant OS, subject to their requirements.

CLOUD SERVER

Virtual private server (VPS)

A rented server can be convenient when a service needs to be reachable online. You’ll still need to manage updates, access, and backups.

Examples to exploreVaultwarden, Uptime Kuma, Gitea, Portainer, Nginx Proxy Manager, and Traefik.

Media and photo libraries depend on available storage, transfer limits, and bandwidth.

A note before you begin

Running it means looking after it.

Self-hosting gives you more control, but it also gives you more responsibility. Keep software current, use strong unique credentials, restrict access to what you need, and make backups that you’ve tested restoring.

A password manager, file server, or remotely accessible service can contain sensitive data. Read the project’s security guidance and understand the risks before putting sensitive information online.
